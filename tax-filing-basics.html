Tax Filing Basics

A Complete Guide to Understanding Tax Filing Requirements

Published: January 2026 | 8 min read | Tax Education

Filing taxes can seem overwhelming, but understanding the basics makes the process much more manageable. Whether you're filing for the first time or looking to refresh your knowledge, this guide covers the essential information you need to know about tax filing in the United States and specifically for Pennsylvania residents.

What is Tax Filing?

Tax filing is the process of submitting your tax return to the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) and, if applicable, to your state's Department of Revenue. Your tax return reports your income, expenses, and other financial information for the previous tax year. Based on this information, the government calculates how much tax you owe or how much of a refund you should receive.

Most U.S. citizens and residents are required to file a federal tax return annually. The deadline for filing is typically April 15th, unless it falls on a weekend or holiday, in which case the deadline is extended to the next business day.

Who Must File Taxes?

Not everyone is required to file a tax return. The IRS sets income thresholds that determine whether filing is mandatory. These thresholds depend on your filing status, age, and the type of income you receive. Generally, you must file if:

  • Your gross income exceeds the standard deduction for your filing status
  • You're self-employed and earned $400 or more
  • You owe alternative minimum tax
  • You owe household employment taxes
  • You received advance payments of the premium tax credit
  • You're claiming certain tax credits that require filing

Even if you're not required to file, you may want to file if you had taxes withheld from your paycheck and could be eligible for a refund.

Essential Documents You Need

Before you begin preparing your tax return, gather all necessary documents. Having everything organized beforehand will make the process smoother and help ensure accuracy. Key documents include:

Income Documents

  • W-2 Forms: Provided by employers showing wages earned and taxes withheld
  • 1099 Forms: Various types for different income sources:
    • 1099-INT: Interest income from banks
    • 1099-DIV: Dividend income from investments
    • 1099-MISC: Miscellaneous income for freelancers and contractors
    • 1099-NEC: Nonemployee compensation (replaced 1099-MISC for contractor work)
    • 1099-G: Government payments including unemployment benefits
    • 1099-R: Distributions from pensions, annuities, and retirement plans
  • K-1 Forms: For partnerships, S corporations, and trusts

Deduction and Credit Documents

  • Form 1098: Mortgage interest statement
  • Property tax records: For claiming property tax deductions
  • Charitable donation receipts: For claiming charitable contribution deductions
  • Medical expense records: If claiming medical expense deductions
  • Education expense documents: Form 1098-T for tuition, records for student loan interest
  • Child care expense records: For claiming child and dependent care credits

Filing Status Options

Your filing status affects your tax rate, standard deduction, and eligibility for certain tax credits. The five filing statuses are:

  • Single: For unmarried individuals who don't qualify for another status
  • Married Filing Jointly: For married couples who file one return together (often results in lower taxes)
  • Married Filing Separately: For married couples who choose to file separate returns
  • Head of Household: For unmarried individuals who pay more than half the cost of maintaining a home and have a qualifying dependent
  • Qualifying Widow(er) with Dependent Child: For surviving spouses with dependent children (available for two years after spouse's death)

Standard Deduction vs. Itemized Deductions

When filing your taxes, you can choose between taking the standard deduction or itemizing your deductions. The standard deduction is a fixed amount that reduces your taxable income. For the 2025 tax year, the standard deduction amounts are approximately:

  • Single: $14,600
  • Married Filing Jointly: $29,200
  • Married Filing Separately: $14,600
  • Head of Household: $21,900

You should itemize deductions if your total itemized deductions exceed the standard deduction. Common itemized deductions include:

  • Mortgage interest
  • State and local taxes (up to $10,000)
  • Charitable contributions
  • Medical expenses exceeding 7.5% of adjusted gross income

Pennsylvania-Specific Considerations

As a Pennsylvania resident, you must also file a state tax return (PA-40) if your income exceeds Pennsylvania's filing threshold. Important Pennsylvania-specific notes include:

  • Pennsylvania does not have a standard deduction - you must itemize deductions
  • Pennsylvania has a flat income tax rate (currently 3.07%)
  • Pennsylvania does not tax retirement income for residents aged 59½ and older
  • You may also need to file local Earned Income Tax (EIT) returns for your municipality

Tax Filing Methods

You have several options for filing your tax return:

  • E-file: Electronic filing is the fastest and most accurate method. Most tax preparers offer e-filing, and the IRS provides free filing options for eligible taxpayers.
  • Paper Filing: You can mail a paper return, though this takes longer to process.
  • Tax Preparation Software: Programs like TurboTax, H&R Block, and others guide you through the filing process.
  • Professional Tax Preparer: Hiring a tax professional ensures accuracy and can help maximize deductions, especially for complex situations.

Common Tax Credits

Tax credits directly reduce the amount of tax you owe and can result in significant savings. Common credits include:

  • Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC): For low-to-moderate income working individuals and families
  • Child Tax Credit: For families with qualifying children
  • American Opportunity Tax Credit: For education expenses
  • Saver's Credit: For contributions to retirement accounts
  • Child and Dependent Care Credit: For child care expenses

When to Seek Professional Help

While many people can file their own taxes using software, there are situations where professional assistance is beneficial:

  • You're self-employed or own a business
  • You have multiple sources of income
  • You experienced major life changes (marriage, divorce, home purchase)
  • You have complex investments or rental properties
  • You're dealing with tax debt or facing an audit
  • You're unsure about deductions or credits you may qualify for

Understanding these tax filing basics provides a solid foundation for managing your tax obligations. However, tax laws are complex and subject to change.
